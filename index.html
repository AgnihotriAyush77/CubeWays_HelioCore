What is SkyQuest?

SkyQuest brings atmospheric science out of the textbook and into students' hands. It is a small, educational weather-observation project inspired by the way real weather balloons collect information from the atmosphere.

THE BIG IDEA ✨

Take the classroom a little closer to the clouds.

Students assemble a compact sensor cube around an ESP32 microcontroller. The cube is designed to be lifted by a helium balloon in a controlled, tethered demonstration. As it rises, its sensors can record changing environmental conditions. Back on the laptop, students can read the measurements, explore graphs and ask questions about the atmosphere.

It is not a toy or a satellite. It is a guided science experiment that helps learners understand how sensing, coding and weather observation work together.

FOR SCHOOLS & EDUCATORS

A guided learning experience — not just a finished gadget.

The kit is intended to help teachers introduce sensors, microcontrollers, atmospheric observations and simple data analysis through a shared classroom project.

Students can ask questions, make observations, write code and explain what their measurements mean.

Safety first: Any balloon lift should be planned and supervised by adults, use a secure tether, stay within site permissions and local rules, and account for weather, payload and safe operating limits. SkyQuest is an educational prototype, not a certified meteorological instrument or an operational weather-forecasting service.
Curious? We love questions.

Quick questions, clear answers.

Is SkyQuest a real satellite?

No. SkyQuest is an educational, balloon-borne sensor-cube concept inspired by atmospheric data collection. It is designed as a ground-built learning kit and a controlled, tethered demonstration — not a spacecraft.

What will students learn?

Students explore temperature, humidity, air pressure, motion, sensors, the ESP32, basic coding, data collection, graphs and the science of the atmosphere.

Can SkyQuest forecast the weather?

The kit can help students observe atmospheric conditions, visualize trends and discuss the fundamentals of weather forecasting. Its educational readings should not be treated as a reliable or official weather forecast.

How do we view the collected data?

The learning workflow is designed around reviewing sensor readings on a laptop through a USB-C connection to the ESP32. The exact logging and communication setup will depend on the final kit configuration.

Who is the kit for?

SkyQuest is being developed for school learning, especially teacher-guided STEM activities for students up to Class 10.
